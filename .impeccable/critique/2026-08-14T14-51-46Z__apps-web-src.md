# Design Critique: the operator dashboard (`apps/web/src`)

Register: product. North star: "The Faceplate" (DESIGN.md, binding). Assessed against the running
app at `http://localhost:5173` with seeded data, plus source.

The craft floor is high. The score is dragged almost entirely by error prevention and error
recovery, which is uncomfortable, because those are the two things PRODUCT.md's own principle 7
makes central.

## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Does this look AI-generated? No.** Clearly human-directed, with an unusually specific thesis.

**LLM assessment.** All six absolute bans are clear, and four were verified programmatically rather
than by eye: no `box-shadow` with non-zero blur cast outside an element anywhere (every non-inset
shadow is a `0 1px 0` hairline); no radius above 3px except lamp glass `50%`; zero untinted hex
greys; no `text-transform: uppercase` outside the four Engraved call sites. No side-stripe borders,
no gradient text, no glassmorphism, no hero-metric row, no card grid, no modal anywhere. Detail is
inline expansion at three nesting levels.

Category-reflex fails at both altitudes, which is the real test. First-order: "webhook ops
dashboard" predicts dark-first, purple-or-blue accent, pill badges, 8px radii. This ships
light-default with a stated scene justification, 2px/3px milled radii, and cyan at hue 195 chosen
for an argued reason: off the red-green axis, so the accent cannot collide with the lamps for the
readers who most need those two meanings kept apart. Second-order: category-plus-anti-references
predicts a Linear/Grafana pastiche. The status **lamp** is a genuine component invention, not a
restyled pill.

The remaining tells are **incompleteness tells, not slop tells**: the design system is more complete
than its implementation. The most telling artifact is `styles.css:800-803`, which paints an input's
border `--lamp-cut` on `[aria-invalid="true"]`, correct and spec'd and **unreachable in two of three
forms**, because only one form ever emits `aria-invalid`.

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs` over 39 files: exit 2, 15 findings, 0 ban / 15 warn.
`em-dash` 11, `pure-black-white` 2, `generic-sans-font` 1, `layout-animation` 1. Nearly all are
false positives against this project's binding spec:

- `pure-black-white` at `styles.css:12` (x2): both columns sit inside a comment *quoting the rule*
  ("no `#fff`, and no `#000`"). Not a declaration.
- `generic-sans-font` at `styles.css:162`: DESIGN.md §3 states "Display Font: none. This system has
  no display face and does not want one" and specifies the system stack verbatim for all six roles.
  The detector's heuristic contradicts the spec.
- `layout-animation` at `styles.css:888`: `transition: left 120ms` on `.switch-plunger`, which
  DESIGN.md §6 explicitly permits, and the box is absolutely positioned so it composites without
  reflowing siblings.
- 9 of 11 `em-dash` hits are in `/* */` comments or are an em dash used as a "no value" glyph
  (`DeliveryDetail.tsx:20`, inside `role="img" aria-label="No status code recorded"`). Only
  `PollStatusLine.tsx:41` and `ChannelDirectoryPage.tsx:29` are em dashes in rendered copy.

**What the deterministic pass caught that judgment missed** is the more valuable half, and it
inverts one of this build's apparent strengths. See Priority Issue 5.

It also found one precise spec-versus-code contradiction of a named rule that neither the live
review nor the detector's own ruleset flagged: `styles.css:76`
`--lip-shade-deep: oklch(40% 0.02 195 / 0.3)`. Hue 195 is right, but **chroma 0.02 exceeds The
Anodized Grey Rule's stated 0.002-0.011 ceiling**, and it is not covered by either written
exemption, both of which are achromatic-only. Every other one of the 45 `oklch` declarations is in
band.

**Visual overlays.** The in-page detector was injected successfully on two surfaces (Channel
directory, and Channel detail Activity filtered to failures) after confirming mutable injection.
Both reported **0 DOM anti-patterns, 0 ban, 0 warn**. The injection also independently confirmed the
50-row failures view. The live server has been stopped.

## Overall Impression

This is a design system with a real thesis, implemented by someone who understood it, and it is
running ahead of its own implementation in the two places that matter most. The Faceplate's
*aesthetic* commitments are essentially fully delivered: the lamp survives color removal, the
mechanical lip reads, depth descends inward, the accent stays quarantined. What has not landed is
the spec's *behavioral* half. The two named rules that govern how the instrument behaves under
pressure, Reward-Early-Punish-Late and No-Flicker, are the two that shipped partially, and the
surface's honesty about state has gaps on exactly the rows an operator is scanning to find trouble.

The single biggest opportunity: **make the leading lamp column tell the truth about health.** That
column's entire stated purpose is being scannable down a list to find what is broken, and today it
says "healthy" on a 100%-failing Endpoint.

## What's Working

1. **The lamp genuinely survives color removal.** Check / cross / slash / clock / turning-ring /
   power / minus, plus lit-versus-hollow glass, plus an always-present legend. `PENDING` (hollow
   clock) and `IN PROGRESS` (lit ring), the two most confusable states, are separated by form rather
   than hue. This is PRODUCT.md's one hard accessibility requirement and it is the one that is fully
   met.
2. **The consequence advisories are the best copy in the app.** "POST /ingest/stripe-prod will
   accept events without an ingest token. The slug is then the only thing gating this Channel's
   fan-out; use a long, unguessable slug (at least 24 characters)" names the exact path, the exact
   consequence, and the mitigation, and it retroactively explains why the seeded
   `public-intake-8f2c41d7a95b46e0b3c9` looks like that. They appear on toggle, not permanently, so
   they cost nothing at rest.
3. **The form language holds under real density without a single template shortcut.** 50 rows, three
   nesting levels of inline expansion, an 8-row Attempt timeline, and nothing reached for a card, a
   modal, or a drop shadow. Both in-page detector runs came back clean. That consistency is
   expensive and it is present.

## Priority Issues

### 1. [P1] The lamp column reports the wrong fact on exactly the rows that are broken

The closest thing to a P0 here, because it defeats PRODUCT.md's first principle ("Health before
detail") and its most frequent job to be done ("confirm at a glance that Channels are healthy").
Three instances of one theme:

- On the Endpoints tab, the Endpoint failing 100% of the time (`0% ok (24h)`) wears a **green Live
  check lamp**, because the leading lamp reports `enabled`, not health. The percentage that
  contradicts it sits in a truncated column.
- The fan-out lamp's `dead_lettered` branch **drops the `X/Y succeeded` suffix that every other
  branch carries** (`StatusBadge.tsx:167` vs 177/187/196), so the highest-stakes row is the only one
  that discards the success ratio: a 4-Endpoint fan-out reads `1 dead-lettered` and hides that 2
  succeeded and 1 additionally failed. DESIGN.md:616's own table specifies it that way, so the spec
  needs the fix too, not just the code.
- For up to 5s after a retry, the parent Broadcast row reports `1 dead-lettered` with a lit Lamp Cut
  while its own child Delivery, visible in the same viewport, reads `PENDING`. The lamp reads
  `["broadcasts", channelId]` (`ChannelActivityTab.tsx:118`) but `DeliveryDetail.handleRetry` (line
  70) only calls `onRetried()` -> `broadcastQuery.refetch()`; nothing invalidates the list key, and
  the 5s poll heals it. DESIGN.md:626 calls this "the one defect this surface cannot afford."

**Why it matters:** the product's whole claim is that it is telling the operator the truth. A lamp
that says healthy on a broken row spends that claim on the exact glance the dashboard exists to
serve.

**Fix:** invalidate the list key on retry (one line). Restore the success ratio to the
dead-lettered legend, in DESIGN.md's table and in `StatusBadge.tsx`. Decide what the constant lamp
column reports for an Endpoint: health, with enabled-ness moving to a switch plate in the row, is
the reading that matches the column's stated purpose.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### 2. [P1] Client-side validation exists in one form of three, and the contract schemas are never imported

Measured live: submitting the New Endpoint form with an invalid URL and malformed headers JSON left
`aria-invalid` `null` on both fields, `aria-describedby` `null`, both borders at `--score-control`,
focus on the submit button, the `role="alert"` element carrying **no `id`** so nothing could point
at it, and **the invalid URL produced no message at all**. There is no `onBlur` anywhere in
`EndpointForm.tsx`. `ChannelSettingsForm.tsx:23,150-155` has the same shape. Meanwhile
`ChannelDirectoryPage.tsx:166,169-174,188-193` does all of it correctly.

The static pass confirms the cause: `apps/web` imports 15 things from `@webhook-broadcast/contract`
and only 3 are value imports; `endpointCreateSchema`'s `z.url()` (`packages/contract/src/endpoint.ts:29`)
is never imported. Worse, `EndpointForm.tsx:100-104` hand-rolls the headers check
(`typeof parsed !== "object" || parsed === null || Array.isArray(parsed)`) where the contract
defines `z.record(z.string(), z.string())`, and the hand-written version is **weaker than the
schema**: `{"a": 1}` passes locally and can only be rejected by the API. That is DESIGN.md:910
("Don't restate a contract constraint as a hand-written check") failing in the one direction that
also costs correctness.

**Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md principle 7 is titled "The server is never the first to say no." It
currently is, for two of three forms.

**Fix:** lift the directory's pattern into a shared field-error hook; import
`endpointCreateSchema` / `channelUpdateSchema` and delete the hand-rolled check; anchor each message
to its field with `id` + `aria-describedby` + `aria-invalid`; move focus to the first invalid field
on submit.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### 3. [P1] Retry promises a budget and grants one attempt, silently

Verified against the worker, not the comments. `retryDeadLetteredDelivery`
(`packages/db/src/repositories/deliveries.ts:331-342`) sets a dead-lettered Delivery back to
`pending` and deliberately leaves `attemptCount` untouched. `processDelivery.ts:134` computes
`n = record.attemptCount + 1` and `:194` dead-letters at `n >= maxAttempts` (8). So a Delivery
dead-lettered at attempt 8 comes back as pending with attemptCount 8, gets exactly one more attempt,
and re-dead-letters unless it succeeds. Observed live: clicked Retry, lamp went `PENDING`, and on
reload it read `dead lettered` again with `41ms`. The operator is told nothing.

**Important correction to the obvious fix:** do *not* reset `attemptCount`. The repository comment
at `:327-330` records why it is left as-is: the `attempt_delivery_id_n_key` unique index forbids
reusing an `n` already written. The backend behavior is intentional and constrained. **This is a UI
honesty defect, not a backend bug.**

**Fix, UI-side:** print the budget in the Attempt timeline (`Attempt 8 of 8`) so spending the last
one is visible before it is spent, and announce the retry's outcome rather than leaving the row to
silently revert. If a Delivery has no attempts left, the control should say so rather than offering
an unqualified "Retry."

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### 4. [P1] No confirm on Replay, the most consequential action in the app

Replay accepts a brand-new Broadcast whose fan-out snapshots the Endpoints enabled *at replay time*,
which `CONTEXT.md:20` notes "may differ from the original accept". So it can deliver a production
payload to Endpoints that were not in the original fan-out. It fires on a single click of a
default-weight control that is visually identical to `Copy`. Bulk Retry, Revoke, and Delete Channel
all have confirm regions.

**Fix:** give Replay the same confirm-region idiom, and have it name the count it will fan out to.
The disable advisory already proves that copy pattern works ("stops fan-out to 4 enabled
Endpoints").

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### 5. [P2] The No-Flicker Rule shipped its visible half and skipped its invisible one

This is where the deterministic pass overturned the live impression. The running app genuinely does
not flicker, and on a fast local fan-out it feels like the rule is fully in force. It is not:

- **The shared hook does not exist.** DESIGN.md §5 requires that `isPending` "passes through the
  shared delay-and-hold hook first, and that hook's output is the only thing components read," with
  "the two thresholds as tokens, not literals." `apps/web/src/lib/` has no such module. The delay is
  implemented in CSS instead: `styles.css:1535`
  `animation: appear-after-delay 1ms linear 225ms forwards`.
- **The delay is 225ms, not the specified 250ms.**
- **There is no 400ms hold anywhere.** No CSS or JS construct implements a minimum on-screen time,
  so any loading state that does appear can still vanish in the next frame.
- **12 control pending states are bound straight to a raw local boolean**, with no delay and no hold:
  `EndpointForm.tsx:202`, `EndpointsTab.tsx:105`, `ChannelSettingsForm.tsx:161`,
  `DeliveryDetail.tsx:166`, `BroadcastDetailPanel.tsx:150` and `:268`,
  `ChannelActivityTab.tsx:287`, `ChannelDirectoryPage.tsx:212`, `ChannelTokensPanel.tsx:68` and
  `:239`, `ChannelDangerZonePanel.tsx:109`. DESIGN.md §6 names this exact case: "Don't exempt a
  control's own pending state from the delay."

**Why it matters:** it currently holds by luck of latency. The rule was written to survive a slow
day, and on a slow day the untimed control labels and the missing hold are exactly what will strobe.

**Fix:** build the hook the spec describes, put the two thresholds in tokens, correct 225 to 250,
add the hold, and route the 12 control labels and the 8 skeleton call sites through it.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

**Sam (screen reader / keyboard):** the worst-served persona. The headers error is a `role="alert"`
with **no `id`**, referenced by nothing, announced as a floating string with no field association,
and it sits *after* the Enabled switch, so tab order reaches the switch, then the error, then submit.
`aria-invalid` never fires on the two forms that need it. The nameless Endpoint row's accessible
name reads its URL twice: "ENABLED https://legacy-erp.acme.dev/webhook/stripe
https://legacy-erp.acme.dev/webhook/stripe default timeout · 0% ok (24h)". Token rows announce
"wbt_tANrsrYX…, 12d ago, 01/08/2026 20:15:58, Revoke" with no word saying what the date *is*.

**Alex (impatient power user):** well served overall, with arrow/Home/End/Enter/Esc traversal, a
documented shortcuts panel, bulk Retry, and URL-addressable expansions. Two breaks: pressing
`Keyboard shortcuts` **moves the trigger itself**, jumping it from x≈1155 to x≈742 and shoving the
filter row down 77px, so the control slides out from under the cursor. And the shortcuts affordance
appears only on Activity, so the same keyboard model is discoverable on one tab of three.

**Riley (stress tester):** found the real one. Retry appears to work and silently produces the wrong
result, which is exactly the "features that appear to work but produce broken results" flag. Also:
submitting `"not a url"` produces no error on that field, so the form appears to accept it and fails
at the server with a message naming no field. Credit where due: unsaved switch and slug edits
**survived** an 8-second wait across two 5s poll cycles. Tested for clobbering; it does not happen.

**On-call operator tracing a failure (PRODUCT.md's own audience):** arrives at the Attempt timeline
having drilled three levels and gets 8 lines reading `request timed out after 15000ms` nine times
over (once as summary, once per attempt), all stamped `23h ago`, with no attempt budget and no
visible backoff spacing. The one screen the product exists for is its least informative. Then the
green Live lamp on the 100%-failing Endpoint sends the same operator's next glance to the wrong row.

## Minor Observations

- **The Endpoint row starves the column the spec says must never yield.** Measured tracks at 1440px:
  `148px | 311.77px | 260px | 237.23px | 27px`. The URL resolved to *exactly* its 260px minimum and
  truncated at 291px wanted, while the **name** track, bare `auto` at `styles.css:1109`, took
  311.77px to hold a 111px string. Cause: `endpointLabel` is `name ?? url`
  (`EndpointsTab.tsx:113`), so the one nameless Endpoint pushes its full URL into a max-content
  track and starves the real URL column for the whole list. The comment at `styles.css:1096-1106`
  claims "the URL therefore takes a pinned minimum and the flexible remainder"; it takes only the
  minimum. The previous fix moved the max-content bully one column left. Fix: `minmax(0, auto)` on
  the name track, and don't render the URL into `.row-name` when it is the fallback, which also ends
  a Machine Voice violation (row 4 prints the same URL twice, once in bold sans, once in mono).
- `ChannelSettingsForm.tsx:142` says "will accept **events**". `CONTEXT.md:18` lists `_Avoid_: event`.
  The only forbidden domain word in user-facing copy, and it is in the most important advisory.
- `styles.css:76` `--lip-shade-deep: oklch(40% 0.02 195 / 0.3)` breaks The Anodized Grey Rule's
  0.011 chroma ceiling and is not covered by either achromatic-only exemption.
- Two em dashes reach rendered copy (`PollStatusLine.tsx:41`, `ChannelDirectoryPage.tsx:29`).
- **Depth stops encoding nesting at level three.** Containers descend correctly (`--face` 0.992 ->
  `--well` 0.94 -> `--well-deep` 0.90), but rows reset to `--face` at every level, so the Attempt row
  measures 0.992 *inside* a 0.90 well: the deepest content in the app is the brightest object on
  screen, sharing its ground with a top-level Broadcast row. DESIGN.md:472 says "never lighter and
  lifted."
- The 404 is the only screen with no plate at all, and it paints its message `--lamp-cut` with
  `role="alert"`. A mistyped URL is not a failure; spending red on an operator typo devalues red for
  the dead-lettered Deliveries it is reserved for, and an alert region announcing on mount is the
  wrong role for static content.
- The empty state prints the literal `POST /ingest/<slug>` while the real ingest URL sits 250px
  above it. Substituting the slug is free.
- New Channel and New Endpoint plates are ~600px in a 1032px column, leaving ~430px of dead ground:
  the exact failure DESIGN.md:751 says narrowing 1180->1080 was meant to fix. The Settings tab
  proves the two-column answer already exists in the system.
- **At 700px the descriptions become fully readable for the first time.** The mid-width layout
  carries more information than the desktop one, because the desktop grid starves the description
  column behind a 220px pinned health lamp. Also, stacked rows lead with `ENABLED`, identical on 6
  of 7 rows, above the identifying slug.
- Tabs are `<button>` in a `<nav>` while rows that navigate are `<a>`. Tabs change the URL, so
  DESIGN.md:670's "what the row does decides its element" argues they should be links. No cmd-click
  or open-in-new-tab on the primary navigation.
- `components/StatusBadge.tsx` still carries "Badge" in its name, in a system whose thesis is that
  lamps replaced pills.
- `styles.css:775` puts full field geometry on the bare `input, textarea` elements. DESIGN.md §2
  names them as Score Control call sites so it is documented, but it is the one place geometry lives
  on an element rather than a class, against the pattern the Controls section argues for.
- `.lamp` (`styles.css:958-970`) carries no `font-variant-numeric`, and fan-out legends print
  compared numbers. It inherits `tabular-nums` from `.activity-fanout` in the Activity list, so it
  is masked there, but any lamp legend rendered outside that container would lose alignment.
- `timeoutMs` has no maximum in the contract (`endpoint.ts:30`, `min(1)` only), so the browser
  cannot warn about an absurd timeout even once it validates.
- The ingest URL wraps mid-token at 400px (`.../inges` / `t/stripe-prod`).
- Not assessed: `LoginPage.tsx` (the `/login` route does not exist; login is a conditional render on
  unauthenticated state, and reaching it means logging out of the seeded session). The Revoke and
  Delete Channel confirm regions, Mint new token's once-only reveal, the Endpoint edit well, and the
  auto-disabled Endpoint's Re-enable advisory were read in source but not seen rendered.

## Cognitive Load

2 of 8 checks failed: **chunking** and **visual hierarchy**. The failures-only Activity view renders
50 rows of which 44 read the identical string `1 FAILED, 3/4 SUCCEEDED`, with nothing ranking,
grouping, or time-bucketing them. The Attempt timeline repeats `request timed out after 15000ms`
nine times in one well, and because elapsed time replaced absolute timestamps, all 8 attempts now
read `23h ago`, flattening the one fact that varied between them: backoff spacing, the thing an
operator reads an Attempt timeline *for*, is no longer visible at all. The other six checks passed,
and no decision point exceeds 4 visible options (3 tabs, 2 filters, <=2 controls per plate).

## Emotional Journey

The **peak** is well placed: the Channel directory deep-links a failing row straight to
`?filter=failed`, so symptom-to-cause starts one click in. The **valley is the terminus**, which is
the worst place for it. An operator who has drilled three levels arrives at 8 identical lines, no
attempt budget, no backoff spacing, and a Retry that buys one attempt and reports nothing when it
fails again.

Reassurance at high-stakes moments is uneven in a revealing way. Revoke, Delete Channel, bulk Retry,
and the edit-swap all confirm, and the delete copy is genuinely excellent, but Replay, the one action
that sends real payloads to a *changed* set of Endpoints, has no guard at all. Separately, the system
has **no form language for "destructive at rest"**: the Quarantine Rule forbids Lamp Cut on controls,
so `Delete Channel` carries the same visual weight as `Copy`, distinguished only by a `Trash2` glyph.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the Quarantine Rule forbids Lamp Cut on controls, what form language does this system have for
   "this control is irreversible"? Right now `Delete Channel` and `Copy` are the same object with
   different glyphs. Is Commit-at-rest the answer, or does the plate need to carry the weight?
2. The Endpoint lamp reports `enabled`, not health, which is a defensible reading of the spec. But
   the constant lamp column's stated purpose is running your eye down it to find what is broken.
   Should that column report the state the operator is *scanning for*, with enabled-ness moving to a
   switch plate in the row?
3. Given that Retry currently buys exactly one attempt, is the `failed` / `dead_lettered` distinction
   still doing work for the operator, or is the honest UI "this Delivery has 0 of 8 attempts left"?
4. Elapsed time was the right call for Activity rows. Was it the right call for an Attempt timeline,
   where every row now reads `23h ago` and the backoff curve, the actual content, became invisible?
5. Failures-only produced 50 rows reading the same sentence. Is a flat Broadcast log the wrong shape
   for the reactive job, and should "trace one failure" start from an Endpoint or a time bucket
   instead?
